## TRANSFORMATION

1. **Effective visibility as a lattice over the ancestor chain, not text.** The issue's two "rules" are shadows of one formalism: rustc gives every import binding an *effective* visibility `meet(import vis, source binding vis)`, and a glob-vs-glob collision is an absorbing *ambiguity binding*. For one module `m`, every legal restriction scope (`pub(self)`, `pub(super)`, `pub(in path)`, `pub(crate)`) is an ancestor of `m` (rustc refuses anything else, E0742), and every binding `m` can access through a glob or import also carries a scope that is an ancestor of `m` or `Public`. So the visibilities that can ever meet in one module form a **chain**: ordered by `within`, meet is the deeper scope, join is the shallower, and the order is total. The current code cannot express this: `Vis` is text, `vis_key` (settle.rs:62-68) sorts `Restricted` lexically, `Origin::widened` keeps an `also` list because it cannot compute a join, and a glob-importer class is a `Box<[bool]>` over the target module's *declared* visibilities (`glob_visibilities`, glob_scope.rs:239-252). The plan's Task 2 says "computing visibility as a meet in the existing fixpoint", but there is no meet to compute on `Vis` text, and a rule-1 meet (e.g. `pub(in crate::x)` inherited from another module's binding) is not among the target module's own visibilities, so `vis_of`/classes would misclassify readers of the re-exporting module.
   Enhancement: introduce `Scope = Public | Module(usize)` resolved once per visibility and home module (`scope_of(&Vis, home) -> Scope`, using the existing `pub(in path)` walk and the `in_path` retry); `Origin.vis: Scope`; `is_visible` becomes `within(importer, scope)`; meet/join are depth comparisons; `also`/`widened` go away (join is total on a chain); a glob-importer class becomes one depth instead of a bit vector (a class of depth `d` accesses everything of depth at most `d`). Rule 1 is then one line (`vis = meet(decl.vis, source.vis)` at the two `vis: decl.vis.clone()` sites, settle.rs:839 and 2232) and is order-independent by construction (meet is monotone in the fixpoint).
   Cost: L (M if staged: `Scope` + meet + `vis_of` extension now, the class-as-depth simplification moved to a tracked change, not a TODO). Payoff: decisive. Both rules become theorems of the data model; the next rustc-fidelity issues in this area (E0364/E0365 re-export legality, `ambiguous_glob_reexports` warn-and-resolve, effective visibility of the export surface) have a structure to land in; the `also` machinery and the per-visibility class vector are removed rather than extended.
   Where: Task 2 ("the new binding carries the visibility of the weaker of the import and the binding it re-exports. Order independence follows from computing visibility as a meet in the existing fixpoint").

2. **Ambiguity as an absorbing binding state, projected per reader by the binding's accessibility, not by each item's.** The root cause of rule 2 is structural: `seen_by` (settle.rs:517-528) filters the items of a `Bound` one by one (`origin.visibilities().any(|vis| is_visible(..))`), and a class `View` holds under each name only the items that class can access (`names: BTreeMap<SharedStr, Lists>`). An ambiguity in `m` whose second item the reader cannot access therefore collapses to the first item. rustc keeps one ambiguity binding and judges its accessibility as a whole. Enhancement: make a name's state in a scope or view a join-semilattice element `Unbound < Bound(items) < Ambiguous{visible items, hidden count}`, with `Ambiguous` absorbing and never reduced by projection; a reader's answer is refused when any constituent is accessible (rustc's E0659 shape) and not-found when none is (the E0573 shape), which is exactly the two outcomes the plan's Task 3 names, but derived once rather than special-cased. This also fixes the export surface, which the Constraints (source-index.md:1677-1682) say lists "the other item" today.
   Cost: M. Payoff: rule 2 falls out of the representation, the surface and `resolve` agree, and future glob-collision rules (warn-and-resolve, shadowing by a later explicit import) have one place to live.
   Where: Task 3 ("the reader gets rustc's answer ... not the other item").

3. **A reusable differential oracle that is the executable form of the Contract's rustc-fidelity claims, with a committed corpus of minimised disagreements.** The plan's Task 1 builds a one-purpose test and Task 4 leaves its results "in the stage notes and the PR", which are not committed artifacts. Enhancement: (a) a generator in `tests/support/` with a feature set (private, `pub(crate)`, `pub(super)`, `pub(in path)` globs; named and renamed `pub use`; items in both the type and value name spaces under one name; enum-variant globs; `self::`/`super::`/`crate::` heads; import cycles; editions 2015 and 2021) so later issues dial in shapes instead of writing a new generator; (b) a typed comparator `Accept(item tag) | Reject` on both sides; (c) a **divergence classifier keyed to the Constraints bullets** (dependency items not found; macro-generated items; `cfg`; the documented `ambiguous_glob_reexports` warn-and-resolve case at source-index.md:1209-1211 and 1682-1686, where the index refuses what rustc accepts), so a disagreement that matches no documented divergence is a bug and the run can reach zero without narrowing the generator; (d) a ddmin minimiser over declaration lines that re-checks the disagreement after each cut; (e) a committed corpus, `fuzz/corpus/rustc-oracle/<name>.rs`, each file with its recorded rustc verdict, item tag and classification, replayed by a `cargo test` that needs no rustc, on the pattern of `corpus_replay.rs` and `support::shapes` (`EXPECTED_NAMES`, "no file is unrecorded"). The live rustc run then only *adds* to the corpus.
   Cost: L. Payoff: R3 ("no new disagreements") becomes reproducible and falsifiable instead of a claim; every disagreement found in Task 4 becomes a permanent regression input; the oracle covers all of `raf-source`'s resolution rules, not these two.
   Where: Task 1 (whole), Task 4 ("results in the stage notes and the PR"), Completeness contract row "re-run the oracle: no new disagreements".

The hand-written every-order fixtures prove the property on two shapes; this proves it on thousands, in the same test binary, at the cost of a second load. Cost: S. Where: Task 1, beside the oracle comparison.

## UTILITY

6. **Oracle item identity and a two-phase compile.** "One compile with error codes per query" cannot tell *which* item an accepted probe resolved to, and rustc aborts after resolution errors, so any type-level identity check in the same compile never runs when one probe fails. Enhancement: give every generated item a unique associated constant (`impl N { pub const TAG: u32 = k; }`) and a probe `const _: () = assert!(<path>::TAG == k);`; phase 1 compiles all probes with `--error-format=json` and takes the resolution verdicts by span; phase 2 recompiles the accepted probes for identity. Use `--emit=metadata`, the edition under test, `-A warnings` (never `-D warnings`: `ambiguous_glob_reexports` is a warning and must count as accept), `$RUSTC` then `rustc`, and record `rustc -V` against `rust-toolchain.toml` in the results. Cost: M. Payoff: the oracle answers "same item" rather than "both accepted", which is the disagreement class the issue actually reports (wrong answers on code rustc accepts). Where: Task 1 ("asks `rustc --crate-type lib` whether `use` probes of each name compile (one probe item per query, in one compile with error codes per query where possible)").

7. **Extend the oracle to the export surface at marginal cost.** A second generated crate that writes `use demo::<path>;` for each candidate surface path, compiled with `--extern demo=libdemo.rmeta`, is a differential oracle for `exported_items`; rule 2 reaches the surface (source-index.md:1679-1682), and the surface has its own documented divergences to classify. Cost: M. Payoff: the surface, which the census and workplan consume, gets the same fidelity check as `resolve`. Where: Task 1 and the Completeness contract.

9. **A known-divergence allowlist is a precondition of R3, not a nicety.** Without the classifier of item 3(c) the oracle will report the documented `ambiguous_glob_reexports` divergence, dependency items and macro-generated items as disagreements, and the only way to zero is to narrow the generator to shapes that avoid them, which hides exactly the private-glob collisions under test. 10. **Every-order fixtures must permute across modules and name spaces.** `every_order` (use_resolution.rs:1398-1433) permutes blocks inside one module; both rule shapes span three modules (the glob target, the re-exporting module, the reader), and the resolver decides each name space apart. The fixtures should permute the module declarations at the root (as the tests at lines 265 and 1772 do) and include a shape where the colliding items are a struct and a function of one name, so the refusal in one name space leaves the other resolved. Cost: S. Where: Tasks 2 and 3 ("Fixture in `use_resolution.rs` in every declaration order").

11. **Rule 1 composes along chains and across routes; the plan must say how.** A binding's effective visibility is a meet *along* a route (import after import) and a join *across* routes to one item (`Origin::widened`, settle.rs:86-106, which keeps every route's visibility today). The plan needs a fixture where one item reaches a module by a `pub use` of a private-glob binding and by a `pub(crate)` glob, in every order, and a chain of two re-exports through two private globs, so the meet-then-join is pinned; item 1 makes both a depth comparison, but either way the test must exist. Cost: S. Where: Task 2.

12. **Record the oracle run where the repository records measurements.** `source-index.md` carries its measured counts in the Contract and Verification sections ("1440 orders", registry-scan maxima); the oracle's program count, query count, zero disagreements by class and the rustc version belong there too, without process wording, instead of only "stage notes and the PR". Also keep the default seed list small enough for `make check`, which runs the tests in debug and release, so the live oracle runs twice per gate. Cost: S. Where: Task 4; Task 5 ("bind the new tests").

## Summary

The plan is sound but not maximal. Items 1 to 3 change its shape: the two rules are consequences of a visibility lattice over the ancestor chain and an absorbing ambiguity state, and the "rustc-oracle fuzz" should be a reusable differential oracle with a committed, minimised corpus rather than a one-off test whose results live in a PR. Items 8 and 9 are gaps that would make the plan's own acceptance criteria false or unreachable as written.
