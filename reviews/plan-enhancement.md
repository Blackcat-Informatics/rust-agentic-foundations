# Plan enhancement review

Reviewed: `.stage/replace-wall-clock-assertions-in-tests/plan.md` and its design source `clock-inventory.md` (issue 21, worktree `21-replace-wall-clock-assertions-in-tests`). Standing constraints: no `.goals` file exists; CLAUDE.md/AGENTS.md rules apply (no timing runs, Linux primary, no fallbacks, hooks on). No item below conflicts with them, so nothing is REJECTED.

The plan is thorough at the inventory level (every site classified, mutants named per test, bindings enumerated). What it under-asks is structure: it instantiates three good ideas (work tallies, a virtual clock, signal-level kill checks) as three bespoke, per-crate, per-test constructions. The items below give each its general form, then the leverage, utility and robustness gaps.

## TRANSFORMATION

1. **One cost-model structure for the workspace instead of three copies of `work.rs`.** The feature ("counts instead of clocks") is a shadow of a *cost semantics*: each performance guarantee is a stated cost function of input size, checked by instrumentation, with the order (constant, linear, product) asserted, not the wall time. The plan copies `crates/raf-source/src/work.rs` into raf-okf and raf-workplan ("`src/work.rs` (copy of raf-source's, own enum)", tasks 1 and 2; inventory line 75 "Copy the module (do not share by `#[path]`...)"). The definitive form is a small workspace member (`raf-work` or similar) used as a **dev-dependency** of each crate: dev-dependencies are linked into a library's `cfg(test)` build, so each crate keeps `#[cfg(test)] use raf_work::add; #[cfg(not(test))] fn add(..) {}` and the production guarantee "nothing outside tests" is unchanged, with no `#[path]` and no `cargo package` problem. The crate holds: a `Kind` trait (each crate keeps its own `Work` enum implementing it), `measure`/`measure_kind`, the ten self-tests once, and the growth idiom as a first-class assertion (`assert_order(Order::Linear, |n| ...)` measuring at three sizes and printing all three counts on failure) instead of `one > 0, three + one <= 2*two` hand-written in every test. It is also the natural home for the seam-level counters the workspace already has but keeps private: `crates/raf-source/tests/support/counting.rs` (`Counting<T: Tree>`, `files_read_once`) and, by analogy, a `Counting<R: Runner>` (today `FakeRunner::calls()` does this job). Counting at seams is cross-crate by construction, which answers inventory risk (6) "Counting across crates is not possible while hooks are cfg(test)-only" for every path that crosses a `Tree` or `Runner`. Cost: M (one new member through deny/reuse/msrv/docs gates). Payoff: no triplicated module and tests, one idiom with one failure message, the next crate and the next three issues in this area get the facility free, and cross-crate counting without a design change. Changes: tasks 1, 2 and the inventory's "Shared rules" paragraph.

2. **Make the runner wait on events, not on a pause schedule.** The plan's seam (`Clock { elapsed, pause }`, pure `verdict` and `next_pause`, inventory R3) keeps the poll loop and makes the test clock advance by each sleep; test 1 then pins "105 looks (31 ms from 1+2+4+8+16, then 98 x 20, then 9)". That pins the pause schedule as a contract of a test named "a process that outlives its limit is killed", and the Stepped clock has to do a second job ("on the first `pause` it blocks reading a FIFO") because synchronisation has nowhere else to live. The definitive deterministic runner is a discrete-event wait: the two `Capture` threads already send on channels; add a watcher that blocks in `waitid(P_PID, pid, WEXITED | WNOWAIT)` (rustix, already used by the existing tests at system.rs:353) and sends `Exited`; the loop becomes one `recv` on a merged channel with the remaining deadline, and the seam is `Clock::wait(&Receiver<Event>, remaining) -> Woken` with the single `recv_timeout` in `clock.rs`. The test clock is a scripted event source: it releases `Deadline` when the handshake event arrives, so the FIFO is just another event and the schedule disappears from production and from the tests. Also removes the up-to-20 ms wakeup latency on every run. Non-Unix keeps the poll loop under `cfg(not(unix))` (minimal effort per CLAUDE.md). Cost: L (production refactor, evidence digests change anyway under task 4). Payoff: a smaller and stable tested surface, mutants that hang today fail at once (no event, no deadline, ceiling of looks), and the "equivalent mutant" in risk (6) (`elapsed: limits.timeout()` indistinguishable because "the last pause lands exactly on the limit") stops being equivalent, because `elapsed` at `Deadline` is a reading, not a sum of pauses. Changes: task 4 and R3. If declined, item 9 is the minimum.

3. **Observe the kill through a pidfd with a complete decision table; the plan's `/proc` check has a race.** R3 tests 3 and 5 assert "for the grandchild `/proc/<g>/status` `ShdPnd` has bit 0x100 (SIGKILL) or state Z/X or entry gone". Between the kernel dequeuing SIGKILL (which clears the shared pending bit) and `exit_notify` marking the task Z, the status reads `State: R` with `ShdPnd: 0`: a correct run can fail. The grandchild is reparented after the leader dies, so the test never reaps it and cannot wait for it with `waitid`. The deterministic form: `pidfd_open(g)` as soon as the pid arrives on the FIFO (before the kill, so pid reuse cannot fool it; `/proc/self/fdinfo/<pidfd>` reports `Pid: -1` once it has exited, no `starttime` comparison needed), then after the runner returns: read `/proc/<g>/status`; `T (stopped)` with no SIGKILL pending is a definite failure, fail at once (the kill never reached a process that only `kill -STOP`ped itself and can leave T only by a signal); Z/X/gone or SIGKILL pending is a pass; otherwise block on `poll(pidfd, POLLIN)`, an event wait that returns the moment the exit completes. Every state is covered and no path reads a clock. Needs rustix `event` as a Unix dev-dependency feature (the crate already has `process`). Cost: S-M. Payoff: removes a flaky assertion from the two tests that carry the group-kill guarantee, and the same protocol serves R5 P1 ("the test binary's pid got SIGKILL"). Changes: R3 tests 3 and 5, R5 P1, risk (6) "`/proc` `ShdPnd` relies on kernel signal semantics".

4. **A repository-wide, compiler-resolved clock ban, with the lexical gate as its hook-time mirror.** Inventory (4) turns the gate into a table keyed by hard-coded crate paths; a fifth crate is uncovered until someone edits the table, and a word list misses `use std::time::Instant as Started` or a re-export. The definitive form has two layers: (a) `clippy.toml` `disallowed-types` (`std::time::Instant`, `std::time::SystemTime`) and `disallowed-methods` (`std::thread::sleep`, `std::sync::mpsc::Receiver::recv_timeout`, `std::sync::Condvar::wait_timeout`, `std::thread::park_timeout`, the socket timeouts), which `make clippy --workspace --all-targets -D warnings` already runs over every crate, test and example, with the one exemption written as `#[allow(clippy::disallowed_types, reason = "...")]` in `clock.rs` where `allow_attributes_without_reason = "deny"` keeps it auditable; (b) the existing lexical gate kept for the pre-commit hook, with its directories derived from the tracked list (`crates/*/{src,tests,examples}`) rather than enumerated, raf-source's wider word list applied as a stricter overlay, and the exemption list asserted non-stale as the plan already does. Root `tests/` and `fuzz/` read no clock today (checked), so the rule can be workspace-wide if `tests/repo_gates.rs` exempts itself (its word table names `Instant`). Cost: S. Payoff: coverage by construction for new crates and aliases; one rule instead of a per-crate table. Changes: task 9 and inventory (4).

## LEVERAGE

5. **raf-okf already has counting allocators; the plan should say why they are not the instrument.** Inventory summary: "Counters: raf-okf and raf-workplan have none." `crates/raf-okf/tests/bounds.rs` has a counting `GlobalAlloc` (AGENTS.md:178 names it) and `tests/alloc_bound.rs` uses `cap`. The issue's R11 names allocation counting explicitly, so a reviewer will ask. The reason work tallies are right for R1 is that the three regressions (a `HashSet` versus a scan of `changes`, `partition_point` versus a scan of blocks, a `HashMap` versus a scan of `values`) do zero allocations per step, so an allocation count cannot see them. One sentence in "Declined" closes this. Cost: S. Changes: plan "Declined" and the inventory summary.

6. **Reuse `tests/support/disk.rs::Scratch` for the nonces, and drop the `$CARGO` wrapper option.** R7 re-describes pid plus `AtomicUsize` for five files (`root.rs`, `git.rs`, `gate.rs`, `exec.rs`, `bound.rs`); the helper already exists at `crates/raf-workplan/tests/support/disk.rs:20-27`, so the task is "make the five files use it", not five re-implementations. R6's "Optional: a counting `$CARGO` wrapper logging each call" is what `FakeRunner::calls()` already is for in-process tests, and for cargo-workplan's subprocess tests it would be a timing-free count of nothing the test asserts; remove the option. Cost: S. Changes: task 8, R6, R7.

## UTILITY

7. **Offer the clock seam publicly and keep R5 as the end-to-end integration test it is.** Disposition R5 P1 moves two real-cargo tests into lib unit tests, renames the subject of the evidence record `gate-kills-a-test-past-its-timeout`, and puts a real cargo build inside `cargo test -p raf-workplan --lib`, because `run_with` is private. A `pub trait Clock` with `SystemRunner::with_clock(..)` (documented, the only API growth) lets `tests/bound.rs` inject the stepped clock, keeps the record's subject and `tests/docs_contract.rs:160` untouched, keeps the warm-build out of the unit tests, and gives cargo-workplan's own integration tests the same virtual time later. The trade is one public type; the plan should weigh it against the subject rename and the unit-test build it currently accepts, and state the chosen side. Cost: S-M. Changes: disposition "Never-ending real-cargo tests (R5): P1", task 7.

8. **Write the coverage follow-up precisely.** `exclusion_for` (coverage.rs:788-801) tests every exclusion against every entry because it marks `matched` for unused-exclusion reporting, so the product is inherent to the current contract, not an oversight; "index the exclusions" must return *all* matches, not the first. Say so in the follow-up issue and in the Verification text, and pin `EntriesJudged == entries` exactly beside the product so the follow-up only loosens one assertion. Cost: S. Changes: disposition "Coverage product (inventory 6)", task 3, task 10.

## ROBUSTNESS

9. **Do not pin "105 looks" in the kill test.** With a stepped clock, `elapsed == 2 s` exactly already fails the `pause.min(remaining)` to `pause` mutant (elapsed becomes 2011 ms), and the removed `remaining.is_zero()` mutant runs past the ceiling. Keep the schedule assertion only in the `next_pause` pure test and give the kill test a look *ceiling*, so a change to `POLL_CEILING` is a one-test change. Superseded by item 2 if adopted. Cost: S. Changes: R3 test 1.

10. **Give `Frozen` a look ceiling.** Risk (6) accepts that "Under a frozen clock, R3 tests 6 and 7 hang on a broken mutant". `Frozen { elapsed, looks, ceiling }` panicking after a generous number of yields (the count is scheduler-dependent, so the ceiling is large, not exact) turns a hang into a fast failure for every mutant, not only the ones the pure tests happen to cover. Cost: S. Changes: R3 "Test clocks".

11. **Name the FIFO and signal mechanics and harden the cleanup.** The plan does not say how the FIFO is made (`rustix::fs::mkfifo` needs the `fs` feature as a dev-dependency, or a `mkfifo` subprocess) nor how `pidfd`/`poll` is reached (item 3). The drop guard "SIGKILLs recorded pids" should also kill the recorded *group*, since a failing mutant in test 3 leaves a stopped grandchild whose pid the test may not have read yet. Cost: S. Changes: R3 "Test clocks", test 1 drop guard.

12. **Keep the raf-source release reason when removing the probe.** Task 1 says Makefile/CI/AGENTS/CONTRIBUTING "no longer mention the release-only probe"; AGENTS.md:159-162 and Makefile:14-17 give two reasons for `make test-release` and only the raf-okf clause goes; the CI `test-release` job (ci.yaml:71-86) keeps its raf-source step; `make test-release` remains a gate in task 10. Make the edit explicit so the raf-source sentence is not lost. Cost: S. Changes: task 1.

13. **State what the issue's acceptance grep will still find.** Disposition "Acceptance grep widened" replaces the issue's `grep -rn "Instant::now" crates/*/tests crates/*/src`, but that grep will still hit `crates/raf-workplan/src/exec/clock.rs` after the change. Record in the plan and the PR that this is the one permitted reading (a reading, not an assertion, as the issue words it) and that the extended gate asserts it is the only one. Cost: S. Changes: dispositions, task 10.

## Not raised

Thread-local tallies: the inventory already notes only `exec/system.rs` spawns threads and no counted path runs on them. The `perf stat` target: declining it for CI is sound. Budgets as watchdogs: CI `timeout-minutes` covers every job; no further watchdog is needed.
